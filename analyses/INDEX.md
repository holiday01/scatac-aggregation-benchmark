# Supporting analyses

Each directory holds its own scripts, result tables and a `REPORT.md` describing
what was run, the numbers obtained and the caveats. Figures are regenerated by
the scripts; no image files are stored in this repository.

| directory | question |
|---|---|
| `depth_spread/` | how does each aggregation strategy respond to an imposed 1–40-fold difference in per-cell totals between cell types? |
| `real_tools/` | what do the documented paths of Seurat, Signac, SnapATAC2 and scanpy compute on the same matrix? |
| `multiome/` | matched RNA–ATAC comparison: agreement with the RNA reference, annotation accuracy |
| `normalisers_and_ci/` | eleven further transforms, bootstrap intervals and paired tests |
| `practice_survey/` | how published pipelines perform the aggregation step |
| `deconvolution/` | NNLS deconvolution error and bias per reference-building strategy (exploratory; not part of the reported analysis) |

Everything below is the record kept while the analyses were run.

## deconvolution/ — done 2026-08-27 (REPORT.md)

RMSE on cell fractions, primary design (bulk = sum of raw counts of 500 held-out
cells → CPM; shared signature genes; 3 seeds): HCC / COAD / LUAD

| reference | HCC | COAD | LUAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw mean | 0.039 | 0.088 | 0.046 |
| z-scaled mean | 0.174 | 0.165 | 0.168 |
| log1p(CPM) mean | 0.069 | 0.143 | 0.097 |
| pseudobulk CPM | 0.063 | 0.150 | 0.063 |
| CPM mean | 0.067 | 0.165 | 0.125 |

**Nuance that must go into the paper.** For deconvolution the reference must be
aggregated with the same per-cell weighting as the bulk it is fitted to: a
raw-count bulk weights each cell by its depth, so the raw-mean reference is the
correctly specified linear model for *cell fractions* there, while depth-normalised
references estimate *fragment share* (n_t × depth_t) — against that truth pseudobulk
CPM is nearly exact (RMSE 0.03–0.05, r 0.96–0.98). With equal-weight bulks (each
cell scaled to 10k before summing) the ranking flips and CPM mean is best. What is
wrong under EVERY weighting: log-mean references (over-estimate the deepest type,
+0.07 to +0.15, expm1 does not repair it) and z-scaled references (r 0.1–0.35).
→ The conference paper's Discussion sentence listing "a deconvolution reference"
among consumers of the argmax should be qualified: the argmax/marker recommendation
(linear-scale per-cell normalisation) does not transfer to deconvolution references
for raw-count bulk; the log/z-scale warning does.

## multiome/ — done 2026-08-27 (REPORT.md)

10x pbmc_granulocyte_sorted_10k (Cell Ranger ARC 2.0.0), 11,209 cells with
RNA-only labels (8 types), gene activity from fragments via SnapATAC2 (proximal
59,385 genes; enhancer-aware 19,871). Data in data/raw/multiome_pbmc10k/ and
data/processed/multiome_pbmc10k/ (new). Depth spread between RNA-defined types
only 1.66x (T_CD4 16.0k -> pDC 26.6k), and pDC is also the rarest and most distinct
type -> depth and biology confounded; do not headline rho(depth, share) here.

- Argmax share (proximal / enhancer): raw mean 34 % / 45 % in pDC (z-scale same);
  log1p(CPM) 23/23; pseudobulk 21/18; depth-matched 22/23; CPM mean 19/18.
  **RNA truth on the same cells: 22.5 % in pDC** -> normalised methods recover the
  RNA level; raw mean inflates it 1.5-2x even at 1.66x spread. Equal-n control
  (112 cells/type, 20 draws) keeps the inflation (26/42 %).
- Genome-wide vs RNA truth: ATAC argmax = RNA argmax on 62-68 % of RNA-informative
  genes; mean per-gene Spearman across types 0.55-0.58. Raw mean lowest, spread
  between methods only ~0.03; enhancer model slightly better.
- Held-out 51-marker panel: 40-41/51 under every method (null 8.6) — does not
  separate methods here; raw mean's misses are all myeloid marker -> pDC (deepest).
- Annotation of held-out 50 % by correlation to references: 0.88 accuracy for all
  methods except z-scaled mean, which collapses (0.15-0.33); with Spearman, CPM
  mean 0.73/0.86 and log means 0.71/0.87 beat raw mean 0.66/0.73.
- Not done: a tumour multiome (no quick fragment-file deposit found); scripts run
  unchanged on any h5 + fragments pair.

## depth_spread/ — done 2026-08-27 (REPORT.md, fig_depth_spread.png)

All cells thinned to the p20 row sum (spread 1.00x), then one type kept deep and
the rest thinned to log-spaced factors, S = 1/2/5/10/20/40; deep type in
{B_cell, NK_cytotoxic_T} (HCC) / {B_cell, T_NK} (LUAD) x 3 seeds = 6 replicates.

Top argmax share (mean of 6; deep type wins /6):

| | 1x | 2x | 5x | 10x | 20x | 40x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HCC raw | 30% (0) | 36% (3) | 48% (6) | 59% (6) | 62% (6) | 68% (6) |
| HCC log1p(CPM) | 31% (0) | 28% (2) | 32% (6) | 38% (4) | 43% (5) | 50% (4) |
| HCC CPM mean | 29% (0) | 29% (0) | 27% (0) | 26% (0) | 24% (0) | 24% (0) |
| LUAD raw | 28% | 69% (6) | 85% (6) | 87% (6) | 92% (6) | 93% (6) |
| LUAD log1p(CPM) | 31% | 54% (6) | 72% (6) | 75% (6) | 81% (6) | 83% (6) |
| LUAD CPM mean | 24% | 24% | 22% | 22% (0) | 23% (0) | 25% (0) |

z-scale within 2 points of raw everywhere; pseudobulk CPM within 1 point of CPM mean.
Held-out markers: HCC raw 19.2/22 -> 11.2/22 at 40x (macrophage 6/6 -> 0.4/6),
CPM mean 19.2 -> 18.5; LUAD raw 24.0 -> 10.3/36, CPM mean 23.8 -> 22.8.
Mechanism: mean log1p(CPM) profile tracks detection rate (rho 0.95-1.00), range
1.04 -> 8.6x (HCC); mean CPM profile exactly 1.000 across types at every spread.

**Caveat for the paper:** the raw mean responds to per-type *mean* depth (it is a
sum), not the median: natural HCC has 1.84x median spread but 2.86x mean spread
(Endothelial_stromal p90 = 48k), which is why the natural matrices sit above the
imposed curve at their nominal median spread. Define spread on the mean, or report
both, in the journal version. Also: log-mean already fails at 2x on LUAD (54 %) and
at 5x on HCC (deep type wins 6/6).

## practice_survey/ — done 2026-08-27 (REPORT.md, survey_table.csv)

28 verified items (14 toolkit functions, 6 deconvolution methods, 8 primary papers;
7 further rows excluded as unverifiable, listed in §4): linear-CPM-mean 10,
**log-mean 8**, pseudobulk-CPM 4, **unstated 3**, **raw mean 1**, z-scaled 1,
bulk-sorted 1 -> **13/28 (46 %) aggregate on a depth-reporting scale or leave it
unverifiable.**

- Sharpest contrast: Seurat `DotPlot` averages `expm1(x)` (CPM mean); scanpy
  `sc.pl.dotplot` averages log values (`groupby().mean()`, no expm1). scanpy's
  `rank_genes_groups` docstring: "this is an approximation calculated from
  mean-log values."
- The boundary is where the paper's Discussion puts it: tools that pre-normalise
  per cell are safe (ArchR scaleTo=10000, Cicero normalize_gene_activities,
  Seurat expm1 family); tools that return counts + a log slot (Signac GeneActivity,
  SnapATAC2 make_gene_matrix, epiScanpy geneactivity) are where the failure enters.
- Trap inside ArchR: `getGroupSE(divideN=TRUE, scaleTo=NULL)` returns per-group
  means of raw counts for Peak/Tile matrices — normalisation is opt-in.
- Practice example: Camp et al. 2026 Sci Adv (ccRCC) publishes "scaled average
  gene activity scores" from Signac with the averaging scale never stated.
- 3 of 8 primary papers are depth-safe only by inheriting an ArchR default.

**Novelty verdict:** holds in the narrow form — no prior work benchmarks the
aggregation scale of scATAC gene activity against depth (nearest: Luo 2024 Genome
Biol clustering benchmark, Kwok 2025 Genome Biol TF-IDF cell-level, Teo 2024 Nat
Commun DA tests). NOT defensible: calling the mean-of-logs pathology unknown — it is
established in scRNA-seq (Lun 2018; Booeshaghi & Pachter 2021; Ahlmann-Eltze &
Huber 2023) and CIBERSORTx requires summation "in non-log linear space".
-> Journal version (and ideally the conference Discussion) should concede the
principle with those citations and make the claim about practice + scATAC sparsity.

## real_tools/ — done 2026-08-27 (REPORT.md, results/all_tool_paths.csv)

R 4.5.2 / Seurat 5.5.0 / Signac 1.17.1; SnapATAC2 2.9.0; scanpy 1.11.5. HCC proximal
matrix, corrected labels. Every documented tool path reproduces the paper's
re-implementation to numerical precision:
- Seurat `AverageExpression()` default = mean(expm1(data)) = **CPM mean** 30.1 %, 19/22;
  `layer='counts'` = raw mean 67.1 %, 15/22; mean of the log layer (only by hand —
  copying the data layer under another name, or rowMeans) = 33.6 %, 19/22;
  `AggregateExpression()` = raw sums: 41.0 % in the LARGEST type (NK/cytotoxic T),
  B-cell markers 1/5 — argmax follows n x depth until normalised
  (`return.seurat=TRUE` -> log pseudobulk CPM 31.6 %, 20/22).
- Signac vignette (`GeneActivity()` defaults on the real fragment file, all 12,029
  cells, GENCODE v38 -> `NormalizeData(scale.factor=median)` -> `AverageExpression`):
  25.7 %, 18/21 = CPM-mean behaviour; its counts averaged: 72.4 % in the deepest
  type, 15/21, macrophage 2/6. Signac's matrix = SnapATAC2 default restricted to
  protein-coding <= 500 kb (99 % identical entries; BANK1 dropped by max.width).
- SnapATAC2 `make_gene_matrix` defaults are **byte-identical to the paper's proximal
  matrix (0 of 79.4 M non-zeros differ)**; tutorial normalisation
  (`normalize_total()` median + `log1p`) averaged = 33.4 % = lognorm_mean.
- scanpy `sc.get.aggregate(func='mean')`, `obs_df().groupby().mean()` and
  `sc.pl.DotPlot.dot_color_df` all equal the paper's lognorm_mean (max diff 4e-6);
  the dotplot's dot-SIZE channel (fraction detected) carries the depth signal even
  after normalisation (MRC1 -> endothelial, CD163 -> hepatocyte).
- ArchR not installed; remains a re-implementation.
-> **Attribution fix for the conference paper's Table 2:** Seurat's own summaries
(AverageExpression, DotPlot) are CPM mean, not log mean; the log-mean column is
scanpy (aggregate, obs_df, dotplot colour, rank_genes_groups), the SnapATAC2
tutorial, and any hand-rolled mean over a log layer.
- Seurat `FindAllMarkers(only.pos=TRUE)` (reduced: ≤500 cells/type, 19,730
  expressed protein-coding genes): significant positive markers per type track
  true depth — Endothelial_stromal 4,634 vs CD4_T 274 (17-fold at equal cell
  numbers), Spearman +0.77 (data layer) / +0.83 (counts); 21/22 canonical markers
  are in their own type's list but only 6/22 reach the top 50 (CD163 rank 813,
  CD8A 1,329). Mirrors the scanpy rank_genes_groups result: normalisation fixes
  the argmax, not the depth-ordered count of significant markers.
